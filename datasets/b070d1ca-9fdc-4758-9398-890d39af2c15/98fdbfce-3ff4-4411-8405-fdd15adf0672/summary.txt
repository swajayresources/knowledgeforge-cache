This paper investigates the use of additive manufacturing (3D printing) to fabricate fibrous sound absorbers. Two methods, fiber bridging and extrude-and-pull, are proposed to print fibers with aspect ratios greater than 100. The sound absorption performance of the printed samples is measured and modeled using the Johnson-Champoux-Allard semi-empirical analytical model.
