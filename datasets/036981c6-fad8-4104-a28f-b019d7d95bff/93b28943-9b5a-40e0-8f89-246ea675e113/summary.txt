This paper reviews the development and application of stealth nanocarriers for cancer therapy, focusing on the comparison between passive and active targeting strategies. It highlights the role of PEGylation in creating long-circulating nanoparticles that evade immune clearance, enabling enhanced accumulation in tumors via the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect.
