The paper examines the widespread, rapid diffusion of transgenic seeds, particularly Bt cotton in India and glyphosate-resistant soybeans in Brazil, through informal farmer networks outside formal biosafety and intellectual property controls. These 'stealth seeds' are saved, cross-bred, exchanged, and sold in an anarchic agrarian capitalism, challenging the effectiveness of biosafety regimes and intellectual property rights, while empowering farmers as active and autonomous agents.
