This paper reviews the physical and chemical factors that affect the attenuation of low-frequency sound in seawater, with a focus on the contributions of chemical relaxation processes involving boric acid/borate and magnesium/carbonate equilibria. The available evidence indicates that below 0.5 kHz, attenuation is predominantly affected by diffraction and diffusive scattering, while in the 0.5-2.5 kHz range, chemical relaxation processes dominate the sound absorption phenomenon.
