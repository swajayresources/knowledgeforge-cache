This paper investigates the use of diffraction tomography (DT) as a superior technique to detect and image realistic invisibility cloaks, which traditionally reduce scattering cross-section (SCS) to hide objects. The authors demonstrate that DT, by exploiting phase and angular scattering information ignored by SCS, enhances sensitivity in detecting various cloak types based on scattering cancellation and transformation optics, and can reveal approximate cloak shapes and sizes.
