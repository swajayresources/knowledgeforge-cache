The paper reports that moth wings exhibit a natural acoustic metamaterial property that reduces ultrasonic echoes used by bat predators, achieving broadband acoustic stealth in the 20–160 kHz range. This effect arises from the intricate array of scales on moth wings, which vary in size and shape to produce a wide range of resonances, contrasting with butterfly scales that increase acoustic reflection.
